WHY ATTEMPTS LIVE ON THE ROW · R3

Rev A counted attempts in the job args, so a manual re-enqueue restarted at zero and a worker crash lost the count. The row is the one place every path reads.

04

Policy contract

class-level DSL
class DeliverNotificationJob < ApplicationJob retry_policy max_attempts: 8, base: 1.minute, cap: 1.hour, retry_on: [Net::ReadTimeout, Provider::Unavailable], # 5xx discard_on: [Provider::InvalidAddress] # 4xx end # jobs without a policy: RetryPolicy::DEFAULT # max 5 · base 30 s · cap 10 min · retry all
  • Removed vs Rev A: the global RETRIES = 25 and retry_on StandardError.
  • Why per class · R4: a digest email can wait an hour; a webhook consumer gives up on its side after 10 minutes. One global schedule fit neither.
05

Backoff · why jitter

RetryPolicy#backoff
def backoff(n) # n = attempts so far ceiling = [cap, base * 2**(n - 1)].min rand(0..ceiling) # full jitter end # n: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 # max: 1m 2m 4m 8m 16m 32m 1h # worst case before :dead = 2 h 03 min
  • Why full jitter · R1: Rev A’s fixed n**4 + 15 s schedule sent every failed delivery back at the same instants, so the provider saw a wall of traffic just as it recovered.
06

Guarantees · open items

rollout: 3 PRs, reworked in place
  • Nothing is dropped silently: every delivery ends sent, failed (4xx) or dead.
  • A replay or duplicate never re-sends a delivery already marked sent (J2).
  • Accepted · R5: a crash between J3 and J4 can send twice. Why accepted: the provider has no idempotency key; a duplicate email beats a lost one.
